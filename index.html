A Swiss tradition, continued

Every era of industrial change, Switzerland has responded the same way: with cooperatives. Hover over each era to learn more.

1899

Banking for the people

When banks served cities but not farmers, Swiss communities pooled their savings and governed their own credit.

1925

Fair access to essentials

When middlemen inflated food prices, Gottlieb Duttweiler drove a truck to the people. Today Migros is Switzerland's largest retailer, still a cooperative.

1941

Consumer solidarity

When wartime scarcity threatened households, consumer cooperatives merged into a national force, putting purchasing power back in members' hands.

1997

Shared infrastructure

When private car ownership proved wasteful, Switzerland pioneered cooperative car-sharing, now 230'000 members strong.

2025

Collective intelligence

When AI became critical infrastructure controlled by a handful of foreign corporations, Switzerland did what it has always done: built a cooperative.

Frequently asked questions

How does membership work?

Membership in Public AI Switzerland means that you are a co-owner with a vote. To become a member, you must first purchase two cooperative shares of CHF 50 each (CHF 100 total). These shares entitle you to vote. From the second year, the annual membership fee to maintain these shares is CHF 50.

Note that voting rights are separate from fees related to member services. The initial membership fee includes a free, one-year subscription to member services. Using Apertus is separate; the chat is free, and API use is billed separately. Any surpluses are reinvested, not paid out.

Why create a cooperative?

Switzerland increasingly relies on foreign AI platforms governed from Silicon Valley or Shenzhen. It's time for a new model, one we collectively own and control.

What's a cooperative?

A cooperative or co-op is a company owned and democratically controlled by its members. Think Migros, Coop, Raiffeisen, or Mobility.

How does this compare to ChatGPT/OpenAI?

Instead of paying CHF 240 a year for ChatGPT, members pay CHF 100 to own a piece of their own AI.

Is the platform already live?

Yes! Try it at chat.publicai.co or the API at platform.publicai.co/docs.
